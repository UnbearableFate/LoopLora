# Independent review — rv-astra, 2026-10-27

## Assessment and evidence boundary

The strongest contribution is a controlled account of **when repeated low-dimensional memory access helps, what shortcuts invalidate that interpretation, and why useful finite walks need not converge**. The evidence does not yet establish a learned end-to-end LLM memory system, universal single-read limitations, or an efficient reasoning architecture. Oracle P1-L remains pending. Its success would establish a reader result under privileged writing, not repair the learned writer.

I inspected the [brief](brief_2026-10-27.md), conventions, [theory, Props. 1–11](../theory.md), [plan/Appendix E](../../plan/recurrent_lora_research_plan_2026-10-26.md), both evidence ledgers, draft including appendices, relevant preregistrations and study reports, selected CSV/JSON artifacts, and reader/protocol/accounting source. The available codegraph index covered only `main.py`; source inspection supplied the missing context. Literature searches covered all requested areas through October 27, including primary sources beyond the existing audit. No models, tests, training, or scheduler commands were run. Results below are inspected records, not independent reproductions; proposals and costs are prospective.

## Evidence: strengths, confounds, and claim limits

1. **Depth helps tested systems; it is not a lower bound.** [S1/S9/S10/B5](../paper/evidence_symbolic.md) distinguish an orthogonal-oracle full pass from factorized systems with positive depth/specificity contrasts but failed hop-1 preservation. Factorized joint M1 gains 22.975 pp on hops 2–3 while losing 35.617 pp on hop 1; its final L4 accuracy is 19.108 pp below unfactorized joint M1. Larger factorized single reads achieve only 0.24–0.93%, below uniform candidate guessing. Their failure does not identify the best attainable single-read predictor. Factorization removes the demonstrated head-subtraction shortcut, not every global graph statistic. Props. 5/10 and S11 already preclude identifying pointer chasing from accuracy or successor swaps alone.

2. **Same rank and equal parameters diverge for B8.** [B3/B6/FR1](../paper/evidence_symbolic.md) and [LP3](../paper/evidence_llm.md) correctly disclose this. Four unshared R/U/Q sets add $9r^2$ parameters when $r=r_{mem}$. At the LLM projection, registered counts are 103,426→112,642 at r32 and, by the same [accounting formula](../../src/looplora/core/accounting.py), 475,138→622,594 at r128: +8.9% and +31.0%. Frozen oracle components require separate trainable counts. The equal-parameter contrast is shared M1 versus B4. B8's worse long-hop accuracy was measured at L4; it is not a scan beyond B8's trained recurrence depth. Fixed-depth training and independently learned writers also confound an interpretation purely about weight sharing.

3. **Narrow CIs do not imply training robustness.** Draft Appendix B accurately describes episode bootstrap intervals conditional on three training seeds. In S3, M1−B5 effects include +9.3, −17.7, +31.7 pp: episode precision cannot remove seed heterogeneity. C777 is reused across P1-S/F1/F2; C888 is reused by a follow-up informed by P2-S. Preregistration of a follow-up does not make reused confirmation independent. F1 also changes training from 15k to 20k steps. New IS/halting/JS sets are a strength. Retain per-seed effects, distinguish exploratory reuse from confirmation, and avoid treating endpoint intervals as simultaneous coverage of every comparison. Hop-1 point preservation is weaker than a confidence-bound noninferiority claim.

4. **Read distortion is observed; its unique cause is not identified.** [LL7–LL10](../paper/evidence_llm.md) and the [raw final R/r128 row](../../reports/p1l-read-fix/runs.csv) give EM 33.5%, clean/read-head accuracy 93.3/19.7%, key top-1 88.5%, and mean maximum absolute key cosine **0.767**. The brief's 0.85 belongs to W5/r32. A classifier trained on clean values failing on reads can reflect gain, covariance, or coordinate mismatch without total information erasure. E16 is below both key dimensions; correlated but independent keys can be interpolated by a regression memory, so this is not an established algebraic capacity ceiling. Raw-read supervision improves normalized error without rescuing r32 EM. Unequal step budgets, one training seed, repeatedly reused validation, auxiliary-loss competition, and clipping prevent causal ranking of all repairs.

5. **The privileged controls answer different questions.** [LL2/LL3/LP1–LP5](../paper/evidence_llm.md): separate probes do not establish a jointly sufficient shared A or unseen-name generalization. D2's 98.7% bypasses the reader and memory. OA supplies the clean target value, bypassing storage as well as addressing; its 84% is not an addressing-only ceiling. The future random oracle still has interference and an entity-value/pair-key alignment problem. In current [answer scoring and prefill](../../src/looplora/llm/protocols.py), all valid question positions read M. Thus L1 exposes multiple parallel memory observations to upper LM layers, not one observation per question. This is compatible with memory-only isolation, but complicates attributing the final answer to one latent trajectory.

6. **Load and stopping failures have specific scopes.** [IS1–IS4](../paper/evidence_symbolic.md) change graph/candidate/query distributions with E; shrinking depth gain is not an isolated interference intervention. Current-answer update rates of 28.6/41.7% remain poor despite low stale-answer rates. [HL1–HL5](../paper/evidence_symbolic.md) reject six tested halters, not adaptive computation generally: selection maximizes aggregate accuracy without enforcing hop-1 preservation, and predicting current correctness differs from predicting the benefit of continuing. Chosen depths hide fallback execution; measured halting latency exceeds fixed L16. Zero certificates in 96,000 episodes concerns a sufficient norm bound, not absence of convergence.

7. **Stability is not semantic correctness.** [JS1–JS6](../paper/evidence_symbolic.md) show overshoot reductions of 8.433/1.244 pp, but residual losses remain −2.833/−1.772 pp and long-hop accuracy worsens. Radius below one at sampled states is neither a fixed-point certificate nor control of nonnormal Jacobian products. Prop. 3's ideal nilpotent transport can have spectral radius zero with norm above one; useful transient amplification is allowed. Its final state is a weighted sum of visited codes, not necessarily a bare terminal code. The draft is appropriately cautious; introductory language must preserve these distinctions.

8. **DPFP is a useful test, with two extra variables.** [DPFP registration](../prereg/DPFP.md) changes raw queries to normalized feature queries as well as changing geometry. Decorrelation cannot receive sole credit without disentangling normalization; proposal 2 diagnoses inference-time gain effects. Memory expands from 4→24 KiB at r32/ν3, and 64→128 or 384 KiB at r128/ν1 or ν3, in FP32 per episode. Equal learned parameter counts do not imply equal state or compute. Keep its registered selection intact; any extra contrasts need a separate lock. Its causal bottleneck wording is stronger than LL10 supports.

## Literature map: what transfers, and what does not

The numbered links identify verified references below. These are reported findings, not independently replicated results. Full methods were inspected for the closest operator/stopping/stability comparisons; abstract-only records are marked **A** in the reference list.

| Work | Finding, relationship, and implication |
|---|---|
| [1 LoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685v2) | Frozen-backbone low-rank adaptation establishes the parameterization baseline. LoopLora's nonlinear memory-dependent recurrence does not inherit static LoRA's mergeability or zero additional inference latency. |
| [2 Ba et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/1610.06258v3) | A fixed fast-weight matrix is repeatedly queried in an inner loop with sustained input. This directly precedes fixed-memory iterative reading; novelty must lie in the restricted adapter, controls, or findings. |
| [3 Schlag et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/schlag21a.html) | Connects linear attention to fast weights, introduces corrective delta writing and DPFP, and measures association limits as feature dimension grows. | [4 Gated DeltaNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06464v3) | Combines forgetting and delta correction in recurrent sequence modeling. It motivates controlled decay/write-gate studies, not an automatic transfer of language-model quality to this frozen adapter. |
| [5 TTT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04620v4); [6 Titans](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00663v1) | TTT makes the recurrent state an online-trained model; Titans couples learned long-term memory with attention. Both broaden writer alternatives, but changing the whole memory architecture would obscure the present failure. Start with a linear diagnostic. |
| [7 δ-mem](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12357v2) | Frozen-backbone compact online memory produces low-rank attention corrections: a particularly close deployment precedent. Its residual uses the pre-decay state, unlike LoopLora's decay-first residual. Do not assert identical updates or novelty for memory-conditioned low-rank steering itself. |
| [8 Generative Adapter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.05877v1) | Generates contextual low-rank parameters in one forward pass. It is an alternative context-to-weights route, not evidence that repeated reads of one fixed associative state are unnecessary. |
| [9 MesaNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05233v2); [10 Kalman Delta Networks](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07816v2) | MesaNet solves in-context regression more fully; Kalman Delta Networks use uncertainty-aware gains with tractable covariance approximations. They motivate testing storage optimization/conditioning before adding feature dimensions; neither guarantees semantic query alignment. |
| [11 MemReasoner](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07903v1) | Iterates queries over compressed contextual memory and optionally supervises supporting facts; its Larimar backbone uses pseudoinverse operations. This is close multi-hop memory precedent, but with encoder/decoder training and different memory access. Compare assumptions, not just accuracies. |
| [12 End-To-End Memory Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.08895); [13 Key-Value Memory Networks](https://aclanthology.org/D16-1147/) | The first learns multiple memory hops; the second separates addressing and returned representations. They support separate key/value objectives while ruling out novelty claims for multi-hop retrieval or that separation alone. |
| [14 Geva et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2021.emnlp-main.446/) | Interprets FFNs as learned keys with vocabulary-oriented values. This suggests checking whether retrieved values remain decoder-compatible; static pretrained FFN memory is not the episode-specific M here. |
| [15 ROME](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05262v5); [16 MEMIT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07229v2) | ROME edits localized factual associations; MEMIT scales edits across many facts. These distinguish storing an association from using it compositionally. Their persistent weight edits are not direct substitutes for a resettable writer. |
| [17 Huginn](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05171v2); [18 Coconut](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06769v4) | Huginn scales recurrent hidden-state depth; Coconut feeds continuous thoughts as subsequent inputs. Neither supplies LoopLora's equal-rank frozen-memory test, and Coconut's sequence-axis computation should not be described as the same recurrence. |
| [19 Relaxed Recursive Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20672v3); [20 Mixture-of-Recursions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10524v3) | LoRA relaxes shared transformer blocks in the former; token routers and KV sharing allocate recurrent depth in the latter. Weight sharing, adapters, and fixed-context reuse already coexist in prior work. |
| [21 Retrofitting Recurrent Depth](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11233v1) | Reports installing an iterative procedure over frozen weights with intermediate supervision; verbal zero-shot transfer is limited. Directly relevant precedent and motivation for supervised transition alignment, not a reason to assume symbolic-to-LM transfer. |
| [22 MixerLoop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18230v1) | Repeats Gated DeltaNet mixers while executing FFNs once and intervenes on later contextual influence. Narrow recurrence is already studied; its intervention logic is more transferable than its system-level efficiency numbers. |
| [23 RecurTrace](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.03379v3) | Adds loop-history memory and predicts whether later depth improves loss. This provides a concrete alternative to current-correctness halting. Its beneficial minimum-depth floor also limits generalization to LoopLora's hop-1 preservation requirement. |
| [24 STARS](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26733v1) | Combines sampled loop depths with a one-step JVP/power-iteration stability proxy. LoopLora instead computes small-matrix eigenvalues. Neither sampled regularization nor a short power iteration establishes global contraction; do not call JS an exact reproduction. |
| [25 DEQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.01377v2); [26 Jacobian regularization](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/bai21b.html) | DEQ targets an equilibrium and implicit differentiation; its follow-up improves solver stability through regularization. LoopLora needs correct finite transport before stopping, a different objective from equilibrium convergence. |
| [27 ACT](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.08983v6); [28 PonderNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.05407v2) | ACT learns differentiable stopping; PonderNet learns a stopping distribution with an explicit prior. Neither is equivalent to a frozen post-hoc correctness head. Their existence limits the scope of the current negative result. |
| [29 Latent multi-hop](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.550/); [30 SOCRATES](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.205/) | The first finds and intervenes on intermediate entities; the second removes shortcuts and finds relation-dependent composition. Together they motivate causal bridge tests and relation-stratified generalization, not universal latent-reasoning incapacity. |
| [31 Lessons from Studying Two-Hop Latent Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16353v4) | The revised work reports failures and successes across synthetic/natural settings. Cite the current title and qualified result, not the earlier “Two-Hop Curse” framing as an impossibility theorem. |
| [32 Why Knowing Both Hops Is Not Enough](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07261v3) | Controlled experiments separate representing the intermediate entity from upper-layer reuse on an unfamiliar second hop. Recurrent-style training helps there; test cross-context code consistency here instead of assuming good T1 probes imply composition. |
| [33 Tuned Lens](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08112v6); [34 J-lens](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/index.html) | Tuned Lens learns coordinate corrections; J-lens averages downstream linearized verbalization effects and validates interventions. Readability alone is not causal use. J-space is not automatically col(B), and this downstream Jacobian differs from JS's transition Jacobian. |

## Ranked proposals by expected information gained per node·h

Ranking reflects uncertainty reduction and cost, not predicted benchmark wins. Thresholds below are proposed decisions to lock before results. “Fresh” means a new episode range used once; use 97.5% paired episode intervals, conditional on training seeds, for the proposed primary contrasts. Existing studies retain their own registered intervals. New writer work beyond registered DPFP is a recommendation requiring a new authorization/registration, not an extension of its stop rule.

### 1. Repair the claim/comparison contract — 0 node·h

**Rationale:** [2](https://arxiv.org/html/1610.06258v3), [7](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12357v2), and [21](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11233v1) constrain novelty; FR1/LP3 constrain equal-parameter language. **Prediction/check:** configuration accounting will confirm B8's extra R/U/Q parameters, including the larger r128 percentage. Compare registered and actually trainable counts for B8, M1, B4; require every principal claim to name the matching comparator, writer, split, and gate. Reserve “equal parameters” for M1/B4. Keep P1-L's registered B8 gate unchanged; a new M1 primary claim needs a prospectively locked supplementary contrast. Preserve P2-S's negative historical gate. Add a comparison table specifying what loops, whether M changes during a query, backbone training, support-KV access, and state/latency costs. **Decision:** publish no learned-LLM or exact-hop-mechanism claim without its missing evidence. **Risk:** a narrower but better-supported paper may remain primarily a mechanistic study.

### 2. Separate storage, query mismatch, and decoding — 0.15–0.30 node·h

**Rationale:** [9](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05233v2)/[10](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07816v2), Prop. 11. Freeze R/r128 and audited W5/r32 encoders. Cache identical K,V,q; compare a 2×3 factorial: delta versus ridge-regression memory; raw learned query, unit-normalized learned query, or true source key. Replace the final-question-position read, holding other token reads fixed, so true-key interventions do not additionally reveal the relation before its text arrives. Add clean-value and zero-memory controls; allow the same train-fitted scalar calibration procedure in each arm. For K with keys as columns, use $M_{ridge}=VK^T(KK^T+\eta I)^{-1}$, with η selected on training data. Fit a separate linear decoder on actual training reads; retain the original clean-value decoder as control.

**Prediction/decision:** ridge with learned queries halves normalized read error and improves frozen-LM EM ≥10 pp on fresh episodes if this read's storage error dominates. Rescue only with true keys instead implicates query alignment; recovery only after read-decoder fitting implicates decoder mismatch. Log write age, order sensitivity using permutations of cached codes, and full-vocabulary EM. **Risk:** substitutions are distribution shifts and privileged keys are diagnostics, not eligible systems. A negative final-position intervention does not exclude problems at other positions; one failing decoder does not establish information loss.

### 3. Predict the value of another read — 0.20–0.40 node·h

**Rationale:** [23](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.03379v3), HL failures and remaining any-step headroom. Freeze oracle and factorized r32 M1 checkpoints, three seeds. Compare existing correctness heads, the same heads with hop-1-constrained threshold selection, and equal-size heads labeled by whether a later state improves target loss by a fixed margin. Only current-state features are available at inference; future losses supply training labels. Keep fixed L1/L4/L16 references.

**Prediction/decision:** on one fresh set, the continuation head gains ≥3 pp on hops 2–3 versus L1 with positive paired CI, hop-1 loss ≤1 pp, and mean executed depth ≤8. Declare latency improvement only if measured end-to-end stopping beats fixed L16. Reject the proposed policy if no validation threshold meets preservation; do not inspect confirmation. **Risk:** useful future states may be unpredictable locally; a mandatory two-read floor could sacrifice hop 1.

### 4. Isolate interference from task difficulty — 0.20–0.40 node·h

**Rationale:** [3](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/schlag21a.html), IS confounds. Use paired fixed target chains, questions, and a fixed answer universe; add off-path facts drawn from that same universe. Cross low/high extra load with low/high key overlap, first using controlled oracle codes, then cached learned codes. Match relevant-fact age/order and include decay-only controls. No reader retraining initially.

**Prediction/decision:** high-overlap distractors reduce the L4−L1 gain by ≥5 pp more than low-overlap distractors at matched load, with a positive paired CI for that differential loss; otherwise withdraw interference as the identified explanation of IS. Report absolute accuracy, raw read residuals, and candidate-set-invariant EM. **Risk:** constructed code manipulations can be out of distribution; learned-code results remain associational unless overlap changes independently of fact semantics.

### 5. Test LM causality and the equal-parameter claim — 0.30–0.60 node·h initially

**Rationale:** [29](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.550/), [30](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.205/), [34](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/index.html). Preregister fresh evaluation of P1-L M1-L4/B4 at r128 before seeing their confirmation results; reuse the trained checkpoints. Keep known name identities for the primary test; stratify by new second-hop combinations and templates, separately from the original distribution. Compare ordinary reads with reads only at the final question position. On ≤256 paired examples, patch a bridge state from a world differing only in the first edge, with both downstream continuations present in the recipient memory. Include norm-matched unrelated-entity and random-direction controls; select probe/step on validation only.

**Prediction/decision:** require M1−B4 ≥3 pp with positive CI and all three seed effects positive for the supplementary equal-parameter claim. A causal bridge claim additionally requires the intended counterfactual answer shift to exceed controls by ≥10 pp with positive CI. Readability without this effect supports decoding only. **Risk:** masking and patches can be out of distribution; a masking loss motivates matched training, not a leakage allegation. Unseen-name evaluation is a separate codebook diagnostic: arbitrary oracle codes for untrained names need not be inferable. Matched position-restricted retraining is a separate conditional **4–7 node·h** study for six full-budget fits.

### 6. Preserve terminal semantics rather than globally suppress dynamics — 0.6–1.2 node·h

**Rationale:** Prop. 3, [21](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11233v1), [24](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26733v1), [32](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07261v3). At r32 compare identical shared readers under: random-depth training; the same plus next-entity supervision through the first terminal arrival; and the same supervision plus terminal answer-margin preservation at later depths. Use the existing decoder, oracle/factorized writers, and three seeds, training graph lengths ≤3. The third loss preserves the terminal decision, not equality to a bare terminal vector. Include existing JS as a descriptive reference; deploy no extra head or oracle label.

**Prediction/decision:** both writers retain hops 1–3 at L16 within 1 pp of L4 while losing ≤1 pp on held-out hops 4–8 versus the matched random-depth arm; require the depth advantage over a same-rank B4 control to remain ≥3 pp with positive CI. **Risk:** privileged process supervision changes the learning problem, and short-chain terminal targets can inhibit longer walks. Failure would favor selective stopping over an absorbing-state objective.

### 7. Conditional writer replacement: covariance-aware regression — 1.5–2.5 node·h screen

**Rationale:** [9](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05233v2)/[10](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07816v2). Proceed only if proposal 2 shows regression helps with learned queries. Compare continued R, online regularized least squares, and its diagonal-covariance approximation at the selected rank, with identical checkpoint initialization/data/steps, initially one seed and 6k additional steps. Keep value/query objectives and decoder architecture unchanged. Use train-only regularization selection; count auxiliary covariance bytes and write FLOPs. Dense covariance adds r² writer state; it can be discarded after support writing in this protocol.

**Prediction/decision:** seek ≥10 pp EM improvement and ≥50% read-error reduction over matched R before any expansion. Confirm the selected arm versus R with three independently trained seeds and matched full budgets on fresh episodes; retain ≥80% T1 EM as the eligibility target before a separately registered learned-reader study. Budget **4–8 additional node·h** only after a positive screen. **Risk:** ill-conditioning, optimizer adaptation, and repeated-key updates; least squares may average contradictory facts rather than implement last-write semantics. Test update behavior before claiming a general writer. A failed diagnostic is a reason not to run this screen.

## Compute and execution discipline

Initial proposals 1–6 total **1.45–2.90 node·h**, excluding already registered P1-L/DPFP. Proposal 7 and conditional retraining are optional, not committed expenditure. Estimates are extrapolations: [P4-S-L](../reports/P4S-L.md) used 0.1444 node·h for 24 heads/traces/evaluation; [IS](../reports/IS.md) used 0.819; [JS](../reports/JS.md) used 1.011. [Read-fix](../reports/P1L-read-fix.md) packed two r128 24,645-step arms into 4,920 allocated seconds, while its audit took 64 seconds. These anchors justify inexpensive diagnostics, not identical throughput for new kernels or gradient probes.

Start only after **2026-10-28 22:00 JST**. Include startup/evaluation and a 10% timing-only smoke; size requests at 1.5× measured runtime. Prefer one-node jobs ≤1 h, with independent work packed only after measurement; never exceed eight nodes. Use a 2 h request only when a positive earlier result and measured timing justify it. Predefine primary contrasts, seed-level effects, stopping rules, and unused confirmation ranges. Do not rerun the full P1-L matrix merely to add diagnostics, and do not repeatedly inspect confirmation while extending training.
