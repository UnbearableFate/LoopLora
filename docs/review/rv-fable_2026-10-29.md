# rv-fable, round 2 (2026-10-29)

Independent review of the LoopLora results obtained since 2026-10-27, written against `docs/review/brief_2026-10-29.md`.
Numbers come from the ledgers (`docs/paper/evidence_llm.md`, `docs/paper/evidence_symbolic.md`), the reports and the
committed CSVs. The depth-by-hop table in §1.3 is my own seed mean from `reports/p1l/r128/accuracy.csv` and
`reports/p5l/r128/accuracy.csv` (primary setting, descriptive, no intervals). Every work in §2 was opened on its arXiv
abstract page; none is in `lit_check.md`, the round-1 reviews or the draft. One round-1 item (LSQ) is revived because
the evidence that closed it was the wrong kind.

## 1. What the results establish, and what they do not

**Established.** In the oracle condition at r = 128, one shared module read four times beats the equal-parameter single
read on hops 2–3 by +21.55 pp [20.38, 22.76] at Qwen3-0.6B-Base (LP11) and +25.78 pp [24.53, 27.03] at Qwen3-1.7B-Base
(LP15), in every seed, with the memory-use check passing (LP7, LP14). The unshared four-read reader B8 fails G1-L at both
scales (LP9, LP16). The best learned writer reaches 60.8 % single-hop EM at E = 16 and 73.0 % at E = 8 against a 0.80
gate (DP1, PD1). Per-step readouts are decoding only (LP5); no causal bridge was found at the final question position
(PC1).

**Not established.** Any learned-writer multi-hop result; where the walk happens; any reach beyond hop 3 at the deployed
depth; anything at r = 32; any scale trend (two points, carried learning rates, LP20).

### 1.1 Why the learned writer stays far below the oracle

Three separable causes, weighted unequally by the evidence.

1. **Semantic key overlap.** Learned keys of distinct facts have a mean maximum |cos| near 0.8 (P1L-writer-fix), where
   16 random unit codes in 128 dimensions sit near 0.1. A linear key map inherits the LM's similarity between names;
   InfoNCE (W2/W5) separates a key from the learned query, not keys from one another, and the decorrelation penalty (RK)
   left r = 32 at 12.0 %, as R. Zahn et al. [1]: semantic keys overlap and recall falls to near chance after a handful of
   related facts; identifier keys do not.
2. **Single-pass store cross-talk.** With correlated keys the decay-first delta rule leaves the triangular coefficients of
   Prop. 11; read error stays 0.63 at R-r128 (DP2). The exact store read with the true key reaches 84.2 %, equal to the
   clean control (RX1).
3. **Query misalignment.** The exact store read with the learned query gives 38.8 % at η = 1 (RX1), no better than the
   deployed 33.5–39.9 %: a perfect store cannot be addressed by the query the LM learned. The value path is fine (clean
   head 93 %, read head 20 %).

DPFP shows which lever moves under training: +27 pp at r = 128 with no reduction of original-space key overlap (DP2), so
the feature map raised retrieval SNR in the kernel sense of Zhong et al. [17] and Krotov–Hopfield [18] rather than
decorrelating keys; the E = 8 retrain (+12 pp, read error 0.36 → 0.27) shows the remaining loss scales with load. My
main critique of the follow-up plan: F4 (LSQ) was closed on RFX, an evaluation-only substitution into checkpoints trained
with the delta store. No frozen substitution moved EM by more than +6 pp, while retraining with a different address map
moved it by +27 pp; an evaluation-only screen cannot predict a trained store (W2 below).

### 1.2 Why B8 fails while M1-L4 succeeds

B8 contains M1-L4 exactly (tie its step matrices), so the −20.6 pp gap at 0.6B (LP12) and −24.8 pp at 1.7B (LP18) is an
optimization outcome under the locked lr 0.003 and 17,000 steps, not a capacity result. The readouts say how: B8 decodes
names only at s = 4 (19 %) and nothing at s = 1–3 (LP5), i.e. it learned a three-layer feed-forward map ending in one
read, whereas M1-L4 decodes the hop-1 entity at s = 2 at 64 %. Weight sharing fits a homogeneous walk: one lookup map, with gradient from every step. The symbolic reversal (B8 above M1-L4 by 3–13 pp, B3/B4/B6) locates the failure in the LLM setting, where the
lookup is a random pair-code map rather than identity. At 1.7B B8 also loses hop 1 (−1.72 pp, LP16). Untested and cheap:
warm-starting B8 from M1-L4 (R2). Until then the paper may say only what it says now.

stop (best L per hop) would give 64/35/24/17/13.5/12/11/10 %. The ledger records only the L4−L1 contrasts (LP12, LP18);
this matrix is descriptive and carries no interval.

### 1.4 What the r = 32 failure means

At r = 32 the readers never used the oracle memory (hop-1 0.07–0.15 %, LP7), yet the LM uses a 32-dimensional memory
when the codes are its own (R 12.0 %, D32-3 39.0 % on T1), each map is learnable alone (D2 98.7 %; pre-tail probes 84–96 %
head, 99.99 % relation), and the shared-code oracle at r = 32 learned something (O-shared 11.8 % against O-rot 0.1 %,
WL1). The failure is an optimization plateau of the composed map A → Q → M → U → B with random pair codes at low rank,
not a capacity bound (16 facts in a 1,024-number state). Amendment B7's "no claim at r = 32" is right; a rank sweep in the oracle
condition would measure optimization, not memory.

### 1.5 What P5L adds and does not

Adds: the equal-parameter contrast on a second backbone with tighter seeds (24.9/26.5/25.9 against 24.5/15.1/25.1 pp); a
higher hop-1 floor (58.7 against 47.9 %) with the same per-hop retention (0.71/0.54 against 0.74/0.52), which says scale
eased the LM-side address and decode maps, not the composition step; the same B8 failure. Does not add: a learning-rate
pilot (B8's failure is confounded with lr at both scales), any learned-writer, causal or readout result, a scale trend, or
hops beyond 3 at L = 4.

### 1.6 Strongest threats

- **Confounds.** One oracle seed: every LLM composition number rests on one random code table. B8 never had its own lr.
  The deployed readers read at every question position, and restricting memory to the final position costs M1-L4 29.9 pp
  (PC2). The adapter cannot chain reads across positions (position t's branch output enters its residual after layer-14
  attention, unseen by any later adapter input): each question position runs its own vertical walk and the frozen upper
  layers aggregate. PC1's null therefore says the walk that matters is not at the final position (E2).
- **Statistics.** Bootstraps condition on three seeds; the seed spread at 0.6B (15.1–25.1 pp) is five times the
  interval width. A seed-level interval belongs beside the episode one. G1-L/G2-L/B6/B7 are uncorrected, as registered.
- **Protocol.** The E = 8 retrain was rule-triggered but E = 8 is not T2's 12 facts; LSQ was preregistered and closed
  without a run.
- **Framing.** "Reaches hop 3" (true at L = 4 only); "learned writer far below the oracle" (three causes); "B8 fails"
  (one optimizer).

### W1. Classify-then-code writer (privileged oracle codes)

**Rationale.** The only reader that composes is the one trained on oracle codes, and the write-side failure is semantic
overlap plus an unlearned lookup (§1.1). A writer that classifies head entity, relation and tail from the fact's hidden
states and emits the oracle's pair and global codes through a softmax-weighted lookup in the fixed tables is a learned
text-to-memory writer whose keys are orthogonal by construction: DiscoLoop's discrete channel [20] on the write side, and
generalized distillation [29] with the codes as privileged information available only in training (cf. [27]). The frozen
P1-L M1-L4 checkpoints are reused. The writer-fix probes bound the hard part: at the pre-tail position the head is
linearly decodable at 84.0 % (full) and 95.6 % (rank 32), the relation at 99.99 %; at its own token the head is the
token itself, so Stage A decides which positions the writer reads.

**Prediction.** With per-fact code accuracy p, hop-h EM at L = 4 is about the oracle value times p^(h+1): with p ≥ 0.95,
hop 1 ≥ 42 %, hop 2 ≥ 29 %, hop 3 ≥ 14 % at 0.6B (oracle 47.9/35.4/18.5), and M1-L4@4 − B4@1 ≥ +12 pp on hops 2–3 with a
learned writer. Falsified if hop 1 stays below 35 % despite p ≥ 0.95 (soft-lookup leakage; compare a hard lookup at
evaluation) or if the gain over B4 is below +5 pp (the oracle reader does not transfer to near-oracle codes).

**Experiment.** Stage A (0.3 node·h): classification heads from write-position states to (head, relation, tail) on T2
training episodes, measured on validation; pick positions. Stage B (1.4 node·h): train the heads with CE plus EM through
the frozen oracle M1-L4, seed 17, T2 with E = 12, train hops 1–3; evaluate L = 1…8 on validation with the P1-L M1-L4, B4
and B3-L4 readers frozen. Decision: hop 1 ≥ 40 % and M1-L4@4 − B4@1 ≥ +10 pp on validation registers a P1L-D candidate for
a three-seed confirmation under the B6 rule on a fresh range; otherwise stop.

**Risks and scope.** A closed-vocabulary writer (768 seen names), to be stated as for the oracle. With head-position
classification the writer is a parser plus code table, thin as "learning" but exactly the control that separates write
errors from the reader's composition. Writer parameters (two 1,024-way heads, about 2 M at 0.6B) exceed the adapter and
are reported as cost. The claim becomes "a learned text-to-memory writer plus a four-read reader composes three hops
inside a frozen LM", not "the LM learned its own codes".

### R1. Stop where the chain ends (evaluation only)

**Rationale.** §1.3: every hop peaks at L ≈ h + 1…h + 2 and decays. The terminal entity has no outgoing (entity,
relation) key, so the read M Q z collapses to cross-talk there, a label-free signature. Symbolic H1–H6 raised hops 4–8
from 44.9 to 64.4 % with a relative-state-change stop; trained halting heads failed (CVH, HL). Fan et al. [24] and Kuo et
al. [23] treat the stop as the design variable; nothing has been tried in the LLM.

**Experiment.** On the existing r = 128 M1-L4 checkpoints at both scales, run L = 1…8 and apply three per-question rules:
relative state change below τ; logit-lens top-1 unchanged for two steps; read norm ‖M Q z^s‖ below τ′. Choose τ on
validation, evaluate on a fresh range. Metrics: hop 1 against its L = 2 peak, hops 4–8 against L = 4, measured latency. **Prediction.** The read-norm rule gives hops 4–6 ≥ 12 % and hop 1 ≥ 55 % at 0.6B. Falsified if no rule
beats the fixed L = 4 profile on hops 4–8 without losing more than 10 pp on hop 1. **Cost.** About 0.5 node·h (six
evaluation groups); a stopping pass over `p1lc.read_states`. The (h, L) matrix itself costs nothing and belongs in the
paper.

### W2. Trained least-squares stores at r = 128

**Rationale.** `ridge_final` is the exact interpolant M k_j = v_j for 16 independent keys, weighted by the learned decay
as in Gated KalmaNet's online ridge regression [3]; VLA [2] shows this recovers recall below the head dimension where the
delta rule collapses; Preconditioned DeltaNet [6] reads the delta rule as the first-order approximation, and K4 as
Kaczmarz sweeps toward the same solution. RFX could not see co-adaptation (§1.1); `docs/prereg/LSQ.md` and `lsq.py` exist
unrun. Gram–Schmidt of the keys at write time would break the address, since the query never sees the other facts; the
ridge store corrects on the store side, so the raw query still works.

**Arms.** RG(η = 1), RG(η = 10⁻²), K4 at r = 128, E = 16, R objective, DPFP budget; U as unit-query control if budget
allows. Metric: validation EM at the final checkpoint, read error, logged Gram condition. **Prediction.** RG ≥ 50 %
(R-r128: 33.5 %) with read error ≤ 0.35; K4 between. Falsified if RG < 40 %: then query misalignment dominates and the
LSQ line closes on trained evidence. Decision (from the LSQ preregistration): ≥ 0.80 is a writer candidate; ≥ R + 15 pp
triggers W3. **Cost.** 3.2 node·h for two arms, 4.6 for three, smokes included; each job ≤ 1 node × 2 h as DPFP. η is
registered before the run; the float64 solve at n = 16 is trivial.

### E2. Single-position reader

**Rationale.** §1.6: each question position runs its own vertical walk and the upper layers aggregate across positions;
PC2 shows the final position alone is not where the trained reader works, and PC1 patched there. Training with reads at
the final question position only makes one vertical walk carry the answer, makes the bridge patch well-posed, and cuts
reader work by the number of question positions (reported as cost, not claimed).

**Experiment.** M1-L4, r = 128, seed 17, reads masked to the final question position in training and evaluation; then
the P1L-C read-position and bridge-patch analyses at that position. Metric: hops 1–3 EM against the all-position M1-L4 on
validation; bridge shift. **Prediction.** Within 5 pp on hops 1–3, and a bridge shift ≥ 10 pp with the interval above 0.
Falsified if it loses more than 10 pp: then the final-token state u does not encode the start entity, and the fix is a
query that attends over the question, not a different reader. **Cost.** 1.1 node·h (seed 17), 3.3 with three seeds. Code:
a read mask in `train.py`/`evaluate.py` (`protocols.final_valid` already isolates the last segment).

### R2. B8 warm start and lr pilot

Initialize B8's step matrices from the trained M1-L4 (seed 17) and train 17,000 steps at lr 0.003 and 0.001; metric:
hops 2–3 EM against M1-L4@4 and B4@1 on validation. **Prediction.** Warm-started B8 ≥ M1-L4 − 2 pp on hops 2–3 (the
failure is optimization, §1.2). If it drifts below M1-L4 by more than 5 pp, the unshared parameterization is harmful here
and the paper may say so. Cost 2 node·h; descriptive only (B8 is not parameter-matched; G1-L stays failed).

### R3. Terminal fixed point: R = 0 and random depth

**Rationale.** Prop. 3: with R = 0 the state is a fixed point once the walk reaches the terminal, so extra steps cannot
erase hop 1; with R ≠ 0 the state keeps moving (64 → 19 % from L = 2 to 8). Chen [22] stabilizes 20+ steps with
final-only supervision and a near-identity gate; Kuo et al. [23] randomize the loop count. Symbolic JS (random L) reduced
overshoot (ΔS +8.4 pp) but mixed-depth training cost up to 20 pp on trained hops (B6), so random depth should be paired
with a terminal-stable reader.

**Arms (0.6B, r = 128, seed 17).** (a) M1-L4 with R frozen at 0; (b) R = 0 with L ~ Uniform{2…8} per batch; (c) the
existing M1-L4 as control; optionally (d) = (a) with identity activation (R4), Prop. 3's reader. Metrics: the (h, L)
matrix; S = Acc₈ − Acc₂ on hop 1; hops 4–6 at L = 8. **Prediction.** (a) halves the hop-1 overshoot (S ≥ −22 pp against
−45) at ≤ 3 pp cost on hops 2–3; (b) lifts hops 4–6 at L = 8 by ≥ 5 pp over control. Falsified if (a) loses ≥ 5 pp on hops
2–3 (R is needed for the lookup). **Cost.** 2 node·h for (a)–(b), 6 with three seeds. Code: freeze R (`sigma_r = 0` plus
a no-grad flag) and per-batch depth sampling.

### R4. M1-lin at r = 128

Symbolic S7: the linear reader trained at hops ≤ 3 reaches hops 4–6 at 98.7/99.9/97.1 % at L = 8 (oracle_orth); the
ReLU reader does not (Prop. 6). The LLM variant exists (`M1-lin`, identity activation) and never ran at r = 128.
**Prediction.** Hops 4–6 at L = 8 ≥ 20 % (ReLU: 13.8/13.5/12.3); hop 1 at L = 8 still collapses (symbolic 26 %). Cost
1 node·h (seed 17). Risk: a few pp lower at L = 4.

### W3. Ridge + DPFP and a learned feature map (conditional on W2)

DPFP raises retrieval SNR [17, 18]; ridge removes write cross-talk; they act on different terms of Prop. 11. `lsq.py`
forbids `dpfp_nu` and `ridge_final` asserts no feature map: about 30 lines. A Hedgehog-style [16] trained MLP map with a
softmax-mimicking objective replaces the fixed DPFP map in a second arm (about 80 lines). **Prediction.** RG + DPFP ≥
max(RG, D128-3) + 5 pp; a pass of 0.80 if the effects add. Cost 1.5 node·h per arm; decision as DPFP (≥ 0.80 is a
flagged candidate for P1L-D).

### E1. Learned-writer reader study

No learned-writer multi-read row exists. With a writer at ≥ 60 % hop 1 and per-hop retention near 0.7, a composing
reader should reach hop-2 EM of 25–30 % against about 5 % for a single read. Arms: writer frozen (W1 if Stage B passes, else D128-3), readers M1-L4, B4, B3-L4 × 3 seeds at 0.6B, T2 with E = 12
(`stage=reader` with a frozen learned writer is supported). **Prediction.** M1-L4@4 − B4@1 ≥ +8 pp on hops 2–3 with
D128-3, ≥ +15 pp with W1. Gate: the B6 rule on a fresh range. Cost about 8 node·h in two 8-node jobs; after W1/W2, not
before.

### W4. Per-relation memory banks

Four relations, decodable at 99.99 %; routing each fact to its relation's bank (MoM [19]) cuts per-bank load from 16 to
about 4 and removes the pairing from the key. State becomes 4 r_m² (reported as cost; rank unchanged). **Prediction.**
With D128-3's features, ≥ 70 % at E = 16 (E = 8 gave +12 pp from load alone). Cost 1.4 node·h; new code. Below W1–W3
because it changes the state shape.

### R5. Annealed bridge supervision

LOTUS [21] supervises latent positions against gold steps; Chen [22] found intermediate supervision hurt depth
generalization, and the readouts show hop-e decoded best at s ≥ e, so a target at s = e fights the reader's own schedule.
If tried, anneal it away as in [26]. **Prediction.** +3 pp on hops 2–3 at L = 4, nothing beyond hop 3. Lowest value;
after R3 only.

## 4. The paper

1. Replace "composition reaches hop 3" by the (h, L) matrix with the oracle-stop row, descriptive. The honest sentence:
   "at the trained depth the reader reaches hop 3; the same checkpoint walks to hops 4–8 at 10–17 % when run deeper, while
   losing hop 1".
2. Must-not-say additions: "the reader cannot go beyond hop 3"; "B8 cannot compose" (it contains M1-L4; warm start
   untested); "an exact store would not help" (F4 rests on evaluation-only evidence); "the recurrence composes across
   question positions" (it cannot through the adapter; aggregation is by the frozen upper layers) and its converse until
   E2 runs.
3. Claim contract as it stands: the equal-parameter contrast in the oracle condition at two backbones; a negative B8 under
   one optimizer; a negative learned writer at 73 %; readouts as decoding; no causal bridge at the final position; no
   scale trend.
4. Statistics: print the three seed effects and a seed-level interval beside each episode bootstrap; say "one oracle
   seed" in the limitations.
5. Related work: add [1–7], [11–15], [20–23] and [32], citing abstracts only, claiming no priority and saying which are
   not studied; [25] supports a remark that one hop per read is a design choice, not a bound.
6. Mechanism wording: each question position performs an independent vertical walk; which position's walk carries the
   answer is untested.

## 5. DPFP-X prediction (recorded before the result)

X256: 62–70 % validation EM at E = 16, below 0.80. Semantic key overlap does not shrink with r [1], DP2 shows the gain
lives in the feature space, and E = 16 is already far below r; the larger feature space (1,536) raises SNR modestly. X17:
63–70 %, a few points above D128-3 because the 1.7B backbone raised the oracle hop-1 floor from 47.9 to 58.7 %, so the
